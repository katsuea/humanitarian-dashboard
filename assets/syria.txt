**Description**

Following the fall of Bashar al-Assad in December 2024, Syria experienced improved humanitarian access, reduced conflict-related constraints and increased returns of refugees and internally displaced people. The political transition and easing of international sanctions created opportunities for economic recovery. However, progress remains fragile and uneven.

Approximately 15.6 million people needed humanitarian assistance. Major problems include poverty, unemployment, damaged infrastructure, inadequate healthcare and water services, food insecurity, displacement, explosive ordnance and protection risks. Sectarian violence, political tensions, climate shocks and insufficient humanitarian funding further complicate recovery. Many returnees cannot access adequate housing, livelihoods or essential services.

**Source**

OCHA, [Syrian Arab Republic: 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (April 2026)](https://reliefweb.int/report/syrian-arab-republic/syrian-arab-republic-2026-humanitarian-needs-and-response-plan-april-2026-enar)
